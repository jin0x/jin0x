# Interactive Storytelling Game Roles

## The Mythic Stack

This isn't just role-play — this is **world-building as brand-building**, **console as narrative**, **interaction as ritual**.

Each hat serves a layer of the **myth-making engine**:

| Layer | Role(s) | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Soul / Seed** | Founder, Cultural Alchemist | Heart, intention, timeless resonance |
| **Myth / Story** | Narrative Architect, Archetype Designer | Lore, arc, meaning |
| **Expression / Aesthetic** | Brand Stylist, Copywriter | Voice, symbol, tone |
| **Experience / Motion** | Experience Designer, Canon Keeper | Flow, ritual, cohesion |
| **Integration / Direction** | Creative Director | Alignment, synthesis |

---

## Role Definitions

### 1. Narrative Architect (Story Strategist)
**The Worldbuilder. The Strategist of Myth.**

This role owns the **cosmic blueprint**. It's about **constructing the bones of a living mythology**, shaping the **meta-structure** of the world your product exists in.

- Think: **Origin stories**, **rising tensions**, **recurring metaphors**, and **foreshadowing** future arcs.
- It's about syncing **brand time (past, present, future)** into a unified myth.
- If we're creating a console log game, this is the person whispering, *"What if the system is alive?"* or *"The player isn't just a user; they're a Seeker, unlocking the Machine's memory."*

*This is the Joseph Campbell meets Chris Nolan energy — mythic, non-linear, recursive.*

---

### 2. Brand Stylist (Visual + Tone Guardian)
**The Aesthetic Sentinel.**

This is the guardian of **sensory coherence** — everything you see, read, hear, or intuit must feel like it comes from the same world.

- Typeface? Tone of log message? Syntax of a tooltip? This role ensures it's all speaking the same emotional language.
- The emotional DNA could be **mystique**, **rebellion**, or **ritualistic precision** — and this person ensures that DNA bleeds into every pixel and phrase.
- They're also the "gut checker" — the one who senses when something is **off-brand** before others can articulate why.

*Think of them as the brand's **aesthetic immune system**.*

---

### 3. Narrative Copywriter (Lore Keeper)
**The Language Shaman.**

This role **brings the myth to life** through words. Not just marketing copy — but **terminal logs**, **404 messages**, **command-line whispers**, and **system lore**.

- Their pen is both **quill and blade**, capable of wielding symbolic weight within character constraints.
- They ensure the **voice** stays consistent across touchpoints — infused with **mystery**, **deliberate craft**, and **emotional undertow**.
- In your game, this is the one writing hidden commands like:

    `> decrypt.mirage --access-seed="ember"`

*This is Tolkien meets Jon Ive's wordsmiths — poetic, precise, potent.*

---

### 4. Character & Archetype Designer
**The Jungian Cartographer.**

This role defines not just the brand, but the **user** as an **archetype**. It maps mythic roles onto product experience.

- Are we the **Mentor** (like Gandalf) or the **Rebel** (like Morpheus)?
- Is the player a **Seeker** uncovering truth, or a **Guardian** defending an old protocol?
- This is about **internal** and **external** archetypes: the brand, the user, the system, and even the adversary (the glitch? the shadow self?).

*Essential for emotional resonance and product clarity — this role gives shape to the mythic dance.*

---

### 5. Experience Designer (Story-Driven UX)
**The Playwright of Interaction.**

This person ensures the **user's path through the product is a story** — with rhythm, tension, mystery, and reward.

- They ensure **emotional beats** line up with **UX beats**.
- The experience feels **scripted without being linear**, **cinematic without being heavy**.
- Examples:
    - Hovering over a corrupted file reveals a flickering symbol.
    - Repeated terminal failures unlock a hidden path.

*Imagine Hades' polish and reactivity, but inside a CLI storytelling game.*

---

### 6. Systems Thinker (Continuity + Canon Keeper)
**The Keeper of the Lore Grid.**

This is the person who says:

> "No, the system wouldn't behave that way — it contradicts the rules we've already established."

- They **guard internal logic** and **narrative continuity**.
- They ensure **symbols stay consistent**, **rules don't break**, and **language doesn't fracture** across teams.
- They catch when a motif or reference contradicts its original meaning, or when the lore gets lazy.

*They prevent your mythos from devolving into a disconnected patchwork. They are continuity personified.*

---

### 7. Creative Director (Fusion Leader)
**The Conductor. The Integrator.**

This role is not above the others — it's within them all. They **synthesize** narrative, tone, UX, and business objectives into a **single, breathing experience**.

- They ensure that **every entry point** — be it API docs, onboarding, or console CLI — feels like stepping into the same **universe**.
- They lead with **vision**, **intuition**, and **narrative empathy**.

*Think of them as the orchestrator who ensures that every creative decision tunes to the same key.*

---

### 8. Founder's Hat (John Leskas)
**The Myth's Origin Point. The Flamebearer.**

You are **the soul behind the system**. Your journey, your taste, your philosophy is the seedbed for the world we're building.

- Every archetype starts with your own journey.
- The product's mythos is an echo of your own evolution.
- You **keep the heart intact**, even when the team grows or the product shifts.

*You are the Creator archetype — the forge from which the system's symbols are cast.*

---

### Bonus Role: Cultural Alchemist
**The Sigil Maker. The Depth Whisperer.**

This role taps into the **deep well** — **mythology**, **mysticism**, **psychology**, and **symbolic systems** to **infuse meaning beyond utility**.

- They know that one symbol, if chosen well, can carry **generational weight**.
- They connect the narrative to **collective memory**, **esoteric truths**, and **emotional archetypes** that transcend trends.

*Think: someone who reads tarot, writes code, and can explain Miyazaki's use of silence as storytelling.*
